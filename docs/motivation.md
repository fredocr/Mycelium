# Motivation: community continuity without constant coordination

Status: product hypothesis, not evidence of an approved institutional pilot.

## The problem

A community may already have talented people, useful relationships, and active messaging groups. Yet its collective knowledge is difficult to use: members do not know whom to ask, volunteers repeat administrative work, and introductions depend on one person remembering the right connection at the right moment.

Mycelium addresses three gaps:

1. **Continuity:** an event, request, or commitment should not disappear when its organizer becomes busy.
2. **Context:** a member should be able to express what they need and can offer without publishing everything to a group.
3. **Follow-through:** a relevant connection should lead to a mutually agreed next step, not another abandoned thread.

These are hypotheses drawn from the founding discussion. Interview members and volunteers before treating them as established findings about any particular community.

## Product thesis

Help existing communities coordinate activities and turn voluntarily shared context into useful, mutually approved connections. Earn permission through utility. Do not demand access to calendars, messages, or contact lists as the price of belonging.

Mycelium is not another social feed, a harvested professional database, or a sales CRM. It should serve members, including those who only want to attend a meetup. Social belonging, mentoring, learning, and volunteering matter alongside professional opportunities.

## First setting

The proposed discovery setting is the Berlin chapter of the 28 Digital Alumni Network. Telegram and WhatsApp are existing channels described by the project initiator. Representatives and volunteers remain responsible for community decisions. Institutional authorization, the operating legal entity, the controller, and the participant pool have not been established by this repository.

Start with one chapter and an invited, voluntary group. Do not import the alumni directory or messaging history. The software should later support other communities without silently combining their member data.

## Three jobs to be done

| Person | Job | Smallest useful result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Volunteer | Organize a meetup without repeatedly chasing logistics | Approved event, RSVP list, scheduled reminders, one clear task owner |
| Member | Find someone who can help with a specific need | A relevant suggestion with a reason, a decline option, and no unwanted disclosure |
| Representative | Keep the chapter moving when volunteers change | A bounded view of pending tasks and aggregate outcomes, not unrestricted private conversations |

## What to learn from Boardy

Boardy's public product description emphasizes understanding a need, finding a relevant person, and introducing them after both sides opt in [S1](sources.md). Adopt that interaction pattern, not an assumed internal architecture. Mycelium adds ongoing chapter activities, member governance, and volunteer continuity. It does not require Boardy accounts or sharing alumni data with Boardy.

A system-mediated introduction is not automatically a personal endorsement. Say "You both opted into this community introduction," not "your friend recommended this person" unless that friend explicitly did so. Employment at a company also does not authorize introductions on that company's behalf.

## Value and adoption

The first benefit is fewer coordination tasks, not an AI conversation. Members can use event information without building a networking profile. Matching is optional. Calendar access is a separate, later choice. A web or email alternative should remain available when someone does not use the preferred chat channel.

The network should not optimize for message count, number of stored facts, profile completeness, or introductions at any cost. Optimize for useful completed interactions and sustainable volunteer effort.

## Testable hypotheses

The following are proposed pilot thresholds, not benchmarks or promises:

- Reduce measured administration time per comparable event by 30% across at least two event cycles, without reducing attendance or accessibility.
- Collect at least 15 voluntary need/offer profiles to assess whether useful complementary connections exist. This is an operational threshold, not statistical validation.
- Manually review the first 10 introduction proposals. Record both-side acceptance, meetings confirmed by participants, and usefulness feedback with response counts.
- Treat unauthorized disclosure as a stop condition. Review opt-outs and unwanted-contact reports before increasing proactive messaging.

Record the existing workflow and time spent before changing it. Small-sample feedback is exploratory. Do not claim causal improvement from a single successful event.

## What success looks like

A volunteer can step away without losing the next event. A member can ask for help privately and receive a useful introduction without surrendering their inbox. Representatives spend more time building relationships and less time chasing reminders.

The core promise: **less coordination work, more meaningful community connections.**
